Manna Care

An empowering, community-building approach to healthcare — a new kind of medical mission.

The medical mission

In Uganda, medical services and preventative care are often inaccessible. In 2021, 350 children died each day due to the often preventable and treatable illnesses of malaria, pneumonia and diarrhea — in part from lack of access to primary health care.

  • 1.6 million people live with HIV.
  • 53% of children under age 5 are anemic; malnutrition is rampant.
  • 25% of females age 10–19 have had a baby or are pregnant.
  • Laboring mothers are turned away if they can’t bring their own delivery supplies.
  • Clinics are often understaffed and medications unavailable.

With 29% of rural Ugandans living in poverty and such extreme lack of access to care, the suffering is very great. Harmony Life’s goal is to face these issues head on and provide long-term health care solutions.

Long-term impact

Our first project is a clinic in Kyamasongi, Uganda, near Fort Portal, plus a guest house and ministry center to support it. Three values remain constant in all we undertake:

01

God centered

Every medical service we build is centered on God first.

02

Lasting presence

Any mission trip we run is designed to leave a lasting presence behind.

03

Empower those we serve

Every project exists to empower the community we serve, not replace it.

We engage the community to determine needs from their own perspective; hire and train locals as community practitioners, modeled on the Alaska Community Health Aide Program; partner with local universities for on-the-job clinical training; send periodic Medical Mission Teams; and support local practitioners indefinitely through telehealth “medical traffic” — U.S. clinicians reviewing cases and guiding treatment day to day.

Land secured

Rohlwing Hills Farm.

We’ve acquired nine plots of land in Kyamasongi — enough ground for the permanent clinic, guest house and ministry center this project needs. We’ve named it Rohlwing Hills Farm in memory of Pastor Dan Rohlwing, a friend of Harmony Life who passed away in March 2019.

Maternal health

Mama Kits

Laboring mothers in rural Uganda are often turned away from hospitals if they can’t bring their own delivery supplies. Through our partnership with Mercy for Mamas, we distribute “Mama Kits” and hold monthly trainings for expectant mothers, led by volunteer medical professionals.
